# Commander's Critical Information Requirements

## Operating Summary

A CCIR is an information requirement tied directly to a decision and a reporting requirement.
Only the principal authors and designates the secretary's CCIR list.
The secretary may nominate candidates.
Everything not designated is batchable by construction.
The list must remain short, current, time-sensitive, and auditable.

## Source-Led Facts

- Army doctrine defines a CCIR as information identified by the commander as critical to timely decision-making ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- A CCIR is directly tied to a decision ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- A CCIR establishes a reporting requirement ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- Commanders determine their own CCIRs ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- Staff may nominate candidate CCIRs ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- A CCIR applies only to the commander who specified it ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- A CCIR is situation-dependent ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- A CCIR links to a current or future mission ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- A CCIR is time-sensitive ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- Doctrine warns commanders to limit the number ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- PIR concerns the external world ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- FFIR concerns own-side status ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- EEFI is the mirror category for information that must not leak ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- Current doctrine says rapid reporting is essential ([Army Publishing Directorate](https://armypubs.army.mil)).
- The digest records that a stronger commonly repeated phrase is absent from current editions.

## Operating Procedure

This secretary workflow is digest-labelled encoding of the doctrine.

1. Start with a specific pending principal decision.
2. Ask what information would materially change its timing or outcome.
3. Draft a candidate requirement.
4. Tie the candidate to that decision explicitly.
5. State the reporting condition.
6. State the time sensitivity.
7. Classify it as external-world status or own-side status where useful.
8. Identify related information that must not leak.
9. Nominate the candidate to the principal.
10. Do not designate it on the principal's behalf.
11. Activate it only after principal designation.
12. Keep it applicable only to that principal.
13. Review it when the situation changes.
14. Remove it when the decision closes.
15. Keep the active list short.
16. Batch information that is not designated.
17. Report a trigger rapidly through the declared route.
18. Include the linked decision in the report.
19. Include any information restriction.
20. Coordinate the delivery with [[communication/No-Surprises Protocol]].

### Audit questions

Which principal designated this item?
Which pending decision does it serve?
What exact condition triggers reporting?
Is the item still situation-dependent and time-sensitive?
What information must not leak?

## Boundaries

- The secretary may nominate, but only the principal designates.
- Sender-asserted priority does not create a CCIR.
- The list must not become a general importance catalogue.
- A CCIR without a pending decision is malformed.
- A closed decision should not leave a permanent trigger.
- Rapid reporting does not mean acting without authority.
- EEFI handling must align with [[ethics/Confidentiality and Attribution]].
- A CCIR report can use [[communication/I-PASS Briefing Schema]].
