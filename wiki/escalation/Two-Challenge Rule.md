# Two-Challenge Rule

## Operating Summary

The Two-Challenge Rule makes an ignored concern countable.
AHRQ TeamSTEPPS requires asserting a position or concern at least twice when it is ignored or unheard ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
The first assertion is a clarifying question.
The second adds a supporting statement.
If the concern remains unacknowledged, the challenger must escalate.
CUS is a related trained language sequence.

## Source-Led Facts

- The rule applies especially when a position or concern is ignored or unheard ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
- The challenger asserts the position or concern at least twice ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
- The first challenge is a clarifying question ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
- The second challenge carries a supporting statement ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
- Continued non-acknowledgement creates an obligation to escalate ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
- CUS begins by stating concern ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
- CUS next states why the speaker is uncomfortable ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
- CUS then states that there is a safety issue ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
- “Safety” is a trained trigger word in CUS ([AHRQ](https://ahrq.gov)).
- The digest assigns institutional confidence to these TeamSTEPPS rules.

## Operating Procedure

1. State the concern as a clarifying question.
2. Mark that communication as challenge one.
3. Check for acknowledgement.
4. Stop counting if the concern is acknowledged and addressed.
5. Restate the concern if it is ignored or unheard.
6. Add the supporting statement.
7. Mark that communication as challenge two.
8. Check for acknowledgement again.
9. Escalate if the second challenge remains unacknowledged.
10. Select the route under [[escalation/Three-Way Escalation]].
11. Use [[escalation/PACE Graded Assertiveness]] to make the language grade explicit.
12. Apply [[escalation/Threshold Design]] if a designated override also fires.
13. Preserve the concern under [[failure-modes/Mindguard and Compression]].
14. Use [[failure-modes/Bounded Dissent and Routine Escalation]] for a material policy disagreement.
15. Keep the route routine under [[failure-modes/Bounded Dissent and Routine Escalation]].
16. Use [[communication/I-PASS Briefing Schema]] for a handoff.
17. Reserve one stop phrase that is not used for ordinary messages.
18. Keep that phrase undiluted by routine use.

## Boundaries

- Two challenges do not authorize unsafe or unauthorized action.
- Acknowledgement is not the same as agreement.
- Agreement is not the same as delegated authority.
- The rule requires escalation after two ignored challenges.
- The rule does not specify a vendor priority or SLA.
- CUS is related language, not a replacement for the challenge count.
- The reserved stop phrase must remain exceptional.
- Compression must preserve each challenge and acknowledgement state.
- The principal still decides matters that require principal authority.
