# PACE Graded Assertiveness

## Operating Summary

PACE is a four-step language ladder for increasing assertiveness.
The steps are Probe, Alert, Challenge, and Emergency.
The fourth step is also documented as Escalate.
The digest treats the structure as institutional and the origin as contested.
This note reports the structure without assigning it to aviation or healthcare.
PACE does not replace the countable [[escalation/Two-Challenge Rule]].

## Source-Led Facts

The following structure is also published in an official Australian safety and quality education kit ([primary locator](https://www.safetyandquality.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-04/emergency_triage_education_kit_-_second_edition.pdf)).

- Probe is a clarifying question.
- Alert states a concern and its consequence.
- Challenge states the concern and recommends action.
- Emergency is a direct command.
- Escalate is an attested alternate name for the fourth step.
- The origin is contested between aviation crew resource management and healthcare.
- The digest instructs the brain to report the structure, not the attribution.
- The digest assigns institutional confidence to the structure.
- The digest assigns contested confidence to the origin.
- This note uses contested confidence so it does not overstate the source history.
- The digest itself supplies no primary source locator for PACE, so the locator above was verified separately without expanding the digest-bounded claims.

## Operating Procedure

1. Start with Probe when clarification could resolve the concern.
2. Phrase Probe as a clarifying question.
3. Record whether the concern was acknowledged.
4. Move to Alert when the concern remains.
5. State the concern at Alert.
6. State the consequence at Alert.
7. Record whether the concern was acknowledged.
8. Move to Challenge when the concern remains unresolved.
9. Restate the concern at Challenge.
10. Add a recommended action at Challenge.
11. Record whether the concern was acknowledged.
12. Move to Emergency when direct command is required by the ladder.
13. Use a direct command at Emergency.
14. Record which step was used.
15. Count ignored assertions under [[escalation/Two-Challenge Rule]].
16. Check authority under [[ethics/Agency Authority Boundaries]].
17. Route capability and authority under [[escalation/Three-Way Escalation]].
18. Preserve material dissent under [[failure-modes/Bounded Dissent and Routine Escalation]].
19. Keep the channel normal under [[failure-modes/Bounded Dissent and Routine Escalation]].
20. Use [[communication/I-PASS Briefing Schema]] when handing off the situation.

## Boundaries

- PACE is a language ladder, not an authority grant.
- A direct command does not create delegated authority.
- This note does not choose one origin story.
- This note does not claim an aviation origin.
- This note does not claim a healthcare origin.
- The fourth label is not presented as universally fixed.
- The origin remains contested even though the structure has a verified institutional locator.
- PACE does not replace [[escalation/Threshold Design]].
- PACE does not designate a [[escalation/CCIR]].
