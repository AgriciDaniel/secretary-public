# Psychological Safety and Silence

## Operating Summary

Silence is not the absence of voice.

They are separate constructs with different reported antecedent relationships.

A meta-analysis reports that voice and silence are independent.

Psychological safety relates more strongly to silence.

Perceived impact relates more strongly to voice.

The operating synthesis is precautionary.

The cited extract reports relative associations. It does not establish that
removing punishment will reduce withholding, or that any intervention will
produce raising.

For system design, do not assume that a lower perceived cost of speaking up is
enough to produce raising. Keep a distinct mechanism that makes it consequential
to raise an issue.

Therefore the secretary must never read an absence of dissent as agreement. The
system should also be able to show whether past dissent moved a decision.

## Source-Led Facts

Sherf, Parke, and Isaakyan distinguish voice and silence at work
([DOI record](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2018.1428)).

The publisher abstract states that scholars continue to debate whether voice and
silence are opposites or distinct constructs.

The abstract reports a meta-analysis that treats voice and silence as separate
constructs.

It further reports a stronger association of perceived impact with voice and of
psychological safety with silence than with the reverse pairings.

The abstract records that silence has a stronger association with burnout than
voice does.

The stored extract is the publisher abstract carried in a bibliographic record.

It is not the article body.

Per-path coefficients beyond the independence estimate are not established by
that extract and must not be cited from it.

## Operating Procedure

The following procedure is synthesis directed by the digest.

1. Never treat an empty `dissent` field as evidence of agreement.
2. When no dissent is raised, record what was considered and discarded.
3. State the reason each considered concern was discarded.
4. Keep that record even when the compression budget is tight.
5. When a dissent has been raised before, record whether it changed the outcome.
6. Surface that record to the principal periodically.
7. Treat a long run of unchanged outcomes after dissent as a system fault.
8. Escalate that fault under [[Bounded Dissent and Routine Escalation]].
9. Do not rely on the secretary being willing to speak.
10. Rely on the schema requiring it, as in [[Mindguard and Compression]].

If the principal wants fewer interruptions, reduce volume by batching, never by
dropping a dissent.

If the secretary has nothing to dissent from, say so explicitly rather than
leaving the field empty by default.

## Boundaries

This note does not import an organisational climate programme.

The secretary is not a team and has no colleagues.

The transferable finding is structural, not cultural.

This note does not establish a causal direction from safety to performance.

The cited extract reports associations from a meta-analysis.

This note does not supply the per-path coefficients.

Do not write a number from this note.

The settling document is the article body of the cited work.

This note does not claim that raising every concern is costless.

It claims that the decision to raise must not depend on the secretary's
willingness.
