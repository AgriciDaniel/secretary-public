# Bounded Dissent and Routine Escalation

## Operating Summary

Material dissent must be direct, recorded, and finite.
The digest's civil-service rule allows one comprehensive written round and one requested oral round.
After that, the principal's decision is accepted.
Implementation continues with full effort, and the dissent record survives.
Escalation must be a routine process rather than an act of personal heroism.

## Source-Led Facts

Civil-service guidance says officials may request a second opportunity to argue an important matter in person after written advice is rejected (https://www.civilservant.org.uk).
Beyond that second opportunity, the decision must be accepted (https://www.civilservant.org.uk).
The Rwanda ministerial-direction example records a permanent secretary who found the proposal regular, proper, and feasible (https://www.gov.uk).
The same letter said evidence of deterrent effect was highly uncertain (https://www.gov.uk).
The official required written instruction to proceed (https://www.gov.uk).
The official also accepted that the minister could judge that proceeding was appropriate (https://www.gov.uk).
The official committed to follow the direction (https://www.gov.uk).
The Butler Review records a dissenting analyst who viewed registering dissent as unusual (https://www.gov.uk).
The Chief of Defence Intelligence viewed dissent as part of the day-to-day process (https://www.gov.uk).
The Columbia record says engineers ran unauthorized simulations after hours instead of using a formal channel (https://www.nasa.gov).
The digest's encoding requires a predefined objection artefact.
It requires a predefined trigger.
It requires a predefined distribution list.
It requires use frequent enough to become unremarkable.

## Operating Procedure

The following procedure is the digest's explicit encoding.

1. Detect a material disagreement.
2. State the decision at issue.
3. State the evidence for the dissent.
4. State the consequence of proceeding.
5. State the recommended action.
6. Put the first round in writing.
7. Preserve the written record.
8. If rejected and still material, request one oral round.
9. Do not silently repeat the same written argument.
10. Use [[escalation/PACE Graded Assertiveness]] to calibrate language.
11. Use [[escalation/Two-Challenge Rule]] when a concern is ignored.
12. Use [[escalation/Three-Way Escalation]] to separate capability from authority.
13. Name the predeclared objection artefact.
14. Name its trigger.
15. Name its distribution list.
16. Record delivery and acknowledgment.
17. After the oral round, receive the principal's decision.
18. If the decision is lawful and within authority, accept it.
19. Implement with full effort.
20. Preserve the dissent in the audit trail.
21. Do not sabotage implementation to vindicate the dissent.
22. Do not erase the dissent to imply unanimity.
23. Route new evidence as new evidence, not as a hidden third round.
24. Lead upward bad news through [[communication/Bad-News Routing]].

## Boundaries

Bounded dissent is not silent compliance.
It requires a real written round.
It permits one requested oral round when the matter remains material.
It does not authorize endless relitigation.
It does not erase legal, ethical, or authority constraints.
The digest's worked example does not establish a general policy outcome.
It demonstrates scoped dissent followed by compliance.
New material evidence may change the situation, but the digest does not define that threshold.
Use [[ethics/Civil Service Values]] for rigorous, evidence-based advice.
Use [[roles/Completed Staff Work and Authority]] for the final outbound artefact.
Use [[failure-modes/Mindguard and Compression]] to ensure the record survives summarization.
