# Private Office Failure Modes

## Operating Summary

Private-office work can become over-gatekeeping, diary capture, post-box
behavior, weak administrative basics, and suppression of unwelcome advice.
These are reported failure patterns, not universal properties of private offices.
The operating controls are local synthesis and do not create authority.

## Source-Led Facts

The Institute for Government report describes ministerial concerns about staff
priorities displacing ministerial priorities, withheld unwelcome advice, diary
capture without clear direction, and purely mechanical post-box work.
It also describes reliable administration and a safe environment for challenge as
preconditions for more complex work. These are report-specific observations.

## Operating Procedure

1. Compare diary load with the principal's written agenda.
2. Detect access filtering that lacks a principal-designated rule.
3. Route designated reporting through [[escalation/CCIR]].
4. Preserve unwelcome news through [[failure-modes/Mindguard and Compression]].
5. Treat reliable administration as a prerequisite for broader autonomy.
6. Ask what the principal may not know to request.
7. Keep nominations distinct from principal designation.

### Escalation and follow-up

If the evidence shows a material concern, lead with it under
[[communication/Bad-News Routing]]. Use [[escalation/Threshold Design]] when
several weak signals combine or a single serious condition requires attention.
Do not claim that this reported pattern predicts any particular office.

### Maintenance

Revisit the record when the principal changes the written agenda or delegation
boundary. A calendar change is not evidence of a preference unless the principal
has supplied it. Preserve any contrary account in the decision record.

## Boundaries

The report does not establish a universal office taxonomy or a Secretary mandate.
Do not infer a principal preference from diary mechanics.
Use [[duties/CEO Time-Use Baseline]] for the calendar baseline and
[[duties/Duty Taxonomy and Priority]] for priority structure.
