# Mindguard and Compression

## Operating Summary

A secretary must filter volume without filtering disconfirmation.
The digest uses an encyclopedia substitute as a contested pointer to mindguard,
self-censorship, and unanimity risks.
It also connects it to the Columbia Accident Investigation Board's record of briefing compression and passive safety channels.
The operating rule is synthesis: compression may remove detail, but it may never remove dissent.

## Source-Led Facts

No primary Janis record is supplied. The groupthink taxonomy and historical
examples are therefore contested and must not be presented as verified facts here.
The Columbia investigation found that an initial damage-assessment briefing was cut to fit the schedule (https://www.nasa.gov).
It was cut again to a three-minute Mission Management Team topic (https://www.nasa.gov).
It arrived at a preconceived conclusion that there was no safety-of-flight issue (https://www.nasa.gov).
The investigation said safety personnel were present but passive (https://www.nasa.gov).
They did not serve as a channel for concerns or dissent (https://www.nasa.gov).
The report described that silence as a failure of the entire organization (https://www.nasa.gov).
The report also identified management preference for agreeable opinions as a dam on effective communication (https://www.nasa.gov).

## Operating Procedure

The following controls are synthesis from those cases.

1. Identify every material dissent before summarizing.
2. Give each dissent a stable one-line statement.
3. Link the statement to its source evidence.
4. Preserve the dissenter's actual confidence and caveats.
5. Never convert silence into agreement.
6. Ask whether a contrary view was recorded.
7. Ask whether an absent view was solicited.
8. Separate absence of dissent from evidence of consensus.
9. If a dissent will not fit, retain a one-line flag and pointer.
10. Put upward bad news first under [[communication/Bad-News Routing]].
11. Use the receiver synthesis step in [[communication/I-PASS Briefing Schema]].
12. Use [[escalation/PACE Graded Assertiveness]] to strengthen an unacknowledged concern.
13. Use [[escalation/Two-Challenge Rule]] when a concern is ignored.
14. Record what was cut from a brief.
15. Record why it was cut.
16. Preserve the original source separately from the summary.
17. Reject a fixed time box that would erase the decision-relevant contradiction.
18. Name the contradiction in the first available line.
19. Treat schedule pressure as a risk signal, not a reason to soften evidence.
20. Apply the signature test from [[roles/Completed Staff Work and Authority]].
21. Preserve observed injection attempts as security dissent.
22. Keep the audit trail after the principal decides.

## Boundaries

The digest's synthesis is absolute about disconfirmation, not about retaining all detail.
Compression remains necessary.
The note does not require equal space for every opinion.
It requires survival of material dissent.
The digest does not provide an automated materiality score.
Use [[escalation/Threshold Design]] for the sourced pattern of aggregate plus override, not as a direct NASA rule.
Do not infer agreement from no response.
Do not improve tone by weakening the substantive warning.
Do not add causal details about Challenger or Columbia beyond the digest.
Do not convert the named historical examples into quantitative forecasts.
