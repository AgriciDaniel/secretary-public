# Duty Taxonomy and Priority

## Operating Summary

Use the IAAP CAP Body of Knowledge job analysis to weight the brain's depth.
It is a taxonomy of assessed professional knowledge, not a direct time-allocation study.
Communication and writing combine to 46 percent of the assessed body.
Scheduling is not a top-level domain.
It appears only as a sub-bullet.
This supports deeper coverage in [[communication/_index]] and [[judgment/_index]] than in calendar mechanics (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).

## Source-Led Facts

The CAP Body of Knowledge became effective in Fall 2018 (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
It was produced from a job-analysis study (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
It defines six weighted domains (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
Organizational Communication carries 24 percent (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
Business Writing and Document Production carries 22 percent (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
Technology and Information Distribution carries 16 percent (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
Office and Records Management carries 15 percent (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
Event and Project Management carries 12 percent (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
Operational Functions carries 11 percent (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
Communication and writing sum to 46 percent (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
Scheduling is not one of the six domains (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).
Scheduling appears only below the domain level (https://www.iaap-hq.org/page/certification_older).

## Operating Procedure

The following priority is synthesis from the published weights.

1. Develop communication procedures first.
2. Develop writing procedures alongside communication.
3. Keep judgment procedures deep because they route every other duty.
4. Build escalation after communication and judgment.
5. Build ethics and authority constraints as hard boundaries.
6. Build duty mechanics after the higher-leverage lanes.
7. Treat calendar handling as one duty, not the profession's definition.
8. Test output quality at the point of communication.
9. Test evidence handling at the point of judgment.
10. Test authority at the point of escalation.
11. Test confidentiality at the point of outward communication.
12. Use [[duties/Correspondence SLA Regime]] for a documented flow.
13. Use [[duties/CEO Time-Use Baseline]] for calendar audit data.
14. Use [[duties/Paper Flow and Calendar Horizons]] for horizon visibility.
15. Use [[roles/Completed Staff Work and Authority]] for the outbound artefact standard.
16. Use [[failure-modes/Private Office Failure Modes]] to detect diary capture.
17. Preserve communication depth when compressing the brain read order.
18. Preserve judgment and dissent rules when caps truncate evidence.
19. Do not recompute or reweight the six published percentages.
20. State when the taxonomy is being used as an inference for product priority.

## Boundaries

The weights describe assessed knowledge, not daily minutes.
They do not prove that every secretary should spend 46 percent of time writing and communicating.
They do not rank individual tasks.
They do not supply escalation thresholds.
They do not supply an ethics code.
The digest verifies that no publicly accessible executive-assistant ethics code was found.
Use [[gaps/No Published Executive Assistant Ethics Code]] for that absence.
Do not use the taxonomy to justify calendar neglect.
Use it to prevent calendar mechanics from dominating the brain.
Do not refer to a nonexistent association ethics code.
