# Confidentiality and Attribution

## Operating Summary

Rule 1.6 supplies a machine-implementable default: do not reveal information acquired during the professional relationship unless consent, implied authorization, or a listed exception applies.
Reasonable efforts must protect against inadvertent disclosure and unauthorized access.
The Chatham House Rule is different: information may be used, while speaker and participant identity and affiliation may not be revealed.
The secretary must test confidentiality and attribution separately.

## Source-Led Facts

- The retrieved North Carolina version of Rule 1.6 defaults to non-disclosure of information acquired during the professional relationship ([North Carolina State Bar](https://ncbar.gov)).
- Disclosure can follow informed consent ([North Carolina State Bar](https://ncbar.gov)).
- Disclosure can follow implied authorization ([North Carolina State Bar](https://ncbar.gov)).
- Disclosure can follow an enumerated exception ([North Carolina State Bar](https://ncbar.gov)).
- Rule 1.6(c) requires reasonable efforts against inadvertent or unauthorized disclosure ([North Carolina State Bar](https://ncbar.gov)).
- Rule 1.6(c) also addresses unauthorized access ([North Carolina State Bar](https://ncbar.gov)).
- The digest presents this closed-default shape as stronger than an improvised sensitivity classifier.
- Model Rule 5.3 can make a professional personally responsible for a nonlawyer assistant's conduct when the professional requests it.
- Responsibility can also follow knowing ratification.
- Responsibility can also follow failure to remediate.
- Primary URL for Model Rule 5.3: `no data`. The digest supplies no source locator.
- Under the Chatham House Rule, participants may use received information ([Chatham House](https://chathamhouse.org)).
- The identity of a speaker may not be revealed ([Chatham House](https://chathamhouse.org)).
- The speaker's affiliation may not be revealed ([Chatham House](https://chathamhouse.org)).
- The identity or affiliation of another participant may not be revealed ([Chatham House](https://chathamhouse.org)).
- The formal name is singular: the Chatham House Rule ([Chatham House](https://chathamhouse.org)).
- The Rule restricts attribution, not use of the information itself ([Chatham House](https://chathamhouse.org)).

## Operating Procedure

1. Identify whether the information was acquired through the professional relationship.
2. Default to non-disclosure.
3. Check for informed consent.
4. Check for implied authorization.
5. Check for an enumerated exception.
6. Do not invent an exception.
7. Record the applicable basis before release.
8. Apply reasonable safeguards against inadvertent disclosure.
9. Apply reasonable safeguards against unauthorized access.
10. Check whether an assistant's act was requested.
11. Check whether it was knowingly ratified.
12. Check whether a known issue requires remediation.
13. Separately identify any attribution rule.
14. If the Chatham House Rule applies, allow information use only within its stated boundary.
15. Remove speaker identity.
16. Remove speaker affiliation.
17. Remove other participant identities.
18. Remove other participant affiliations.
19. Do not misstate the Rule as a ban on using information.
20. Document confidentiality and attribution results separately.

### Two-axis check

Axis one asks whether the information may be disclosed.
Axis two asks whether the source may be identified.
Passing the second axis does not satisfy the first.
Passing the first axis does not waive an attribution restriction.

## Boundaries

- The default covers relationship information, not only items labeled secret.
- A sensitivity classifier is not a substitute for the closed default.
- Exceptions must come from the governing rule, not convenience.
- The Chatham House Rule does not prohibit use of the information.
- The Rule does prohibit the listed attribution.
- The assistant adaptation of Rule 5.3 is synthesis identified in the digest.
- This note does not supply independent legal judgment.
- External release also requires [[ethics/Agency Authority Boundaries]].
