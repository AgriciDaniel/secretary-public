# Satisficing

## Operating Summary

Set the aspiration level before searching.

Stop when the first option meets it.

Simon developed satisficing in 1955 and 1956.

He received the 1978 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences.

Schwartz and colleagues found that maximizers reported lower satisfaction and
more regret even when objective outcomes were equal or better.

The digest directs the Secretary to store an aspiration threshold per request
category.

Exhaustive comparison imposes a cost on the principal.

## Source-Led Facts

The named Simon paper has a primary journal record ([QJE](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/69/1/99/1919737)), and the named Schwartz study has a bibliographic record with its DOI ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12416921/)).

The digest traces satisficing to Simon's 1955 and 1956 work.

The digest also identifies his 1956 work.

The satisficing rule searches only until an option reaches a pre-set aspiration
level.

Simon received the 1978 economics prize.

Schwartz and colleagues published the digest-cited satisficing study in 2002.

The digest identifies the journal as JPSP, volume 83, issue 5.

The digest reports lower satisfaction among maximizers.

It also reports more regret among maximizers.

Those results held even with equal or better objective outcomes.

The digest assigns `evidence-based` confidence to satisficing.

The Secretary encoding stores an aspiration threshold by request category.

The digest does not supply exact primary URLs for the Simon or Schwartz works, so the locators above were verified separately.

It takes the first option that clears the bar.

## Operating Procedure

The following procedure is synthesis directed by the digest.

1. Name the request category.
2. Retrieve its pre-set aspiration threshold.
3. If no threshold exists, report `no data` instead of inventing one.
4. Express the threshold as observable acceptance conditions.
5. Establish the order in which candidates will be considered.
6. Test the first candidate against every required condition.
7. Reject it if any required condition fails.
8. Test the next candidate.
9. Stop at the first candidate that clears the full threshold.
10. Record which conditions it met.
11. Do not continue solely to prove that no better option exists.
12. Route one-way decisions through [[Reversibility as the Master Gate]].
13. Route authority through [[Seven Delegation Levels]].
14. Route duration through [[Reference-Class Forecasting]].

The threshold must exist before candidate evaluation to avoid moving the bar
after seeing an attractive option.

That sequencing requirement is synthesis.

If no option clears the threshold, return the failure and the unmet conditions.

Do not silently lower the aspiration level.

## Boundaries

Satisficing does not mean accepting an option that fails the threshold.

It does not mean choosing arbitrarily.

It does not make the first option acceptable by definition.

The digest does not provide Secretary aspiration levels.

It does not provide a universal candidate-ordering rule.

It does not quantify the search cost.

Do not invent a numeric stopping threshold from the studies.

Do not expand the claims from the locator records beyond the digest.

An option that clears a quality threshold can still lack authority.

An option can still be irreversible.

An option can still lose to a more important request under
[[Urgency and Importance]].

An option can still require scheduling under [[ESI Resource Counting]].

Do not apply [[Manchester Triage Architecture]] as though a first discriminator
match were an aspiration threshold.
