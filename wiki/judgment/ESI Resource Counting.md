# ESI Resource Counting

## Operating Summary

Once work is not urgent, sort it by predicted cost to clear.

The Emergency Severity Index separates its two highest levels by acuity.

It distinguishes levels 3, 4, and 5 by anticipated resource count.

The Secretary translation is digest-directed encoding.

Zero-cost items clear immediately.

One-resource items batch.

Multi-resource items get scheduled.

This sorting follows [[Reversibility as the Master Gate]] and the independent
importance assessment in [[Urgency and Importance]].

## Source-Led Facts

The Emergency Nurses Association records its release of the fifth edition in 2023 ([primary locator](https://www.ena.org/news-publications/newsroom/ena-updates-emergency-severity-index-resources-improve-patient-triage)).

The digest cites the Emergency Severity Index, Emergency Nurses Association,
fifth edition.

The digest dates the fifth edition to 2023.

The ESI architecture determines Levels 1 and 2 through acuity.

The architecture determines Levels 3, 4, and 5 through anticipated resource
count.

The digest explicitly records items that do not count as ESI resources.

Oral medications do not count.

Simple wound care does not count.

Crutches and splints do not count.

Prescriptions do not count.

The digest assigns `institutional` confidence to the method.

The digest does not supply an exact primary URL for the ESI handbook, so the official release locator above was verified separately.

The digest's Secretary encoding applies resource count only after an item has
been found non-urgent.

## Operating Procedure

The following procedure is synthesis directed by the digest.

1. Apply [[Reversibility as the Master Gate]].
2. Assess importance without the deadline signal using [[Urgency and Importance]].
3. Resolve any urgent work before using this sorter.
4. Define what it means to clear the item.
5. List the distinct resources expected to reach that state.
6. Count zero-resource work as zero.
7. Count a single-resource item as one.
8. Count work requiring multiple resources as multi-resource.
9. Clear zero-resource items immediately when authority permits.
10. Place one-resource items in the appropriate batch.
11. Schedule multi-resource items.
12. Use [[Reference-Class Forecasting]] to estimate scheduled work.
13. Use [[Seven Delegation Levels]] to verify the decision category.
14. Record the predicted count so later outcomes can be compared.

A resource should be a distinct dependency needed to reach the stated cleared
condition.

The Secretary procedure should avoid inflating the count with ordinary steps
that do not represent additional dependencies.

That last sentence is synthesis, not an ESI definition supplied by the digest.

## Boundaries

This note does not transplant clinical acuity judgments into office work.

It borrows the resource-count architecture only where the digest directs it.

It does not define an office-work resource taxonomy.

It does not assert that zero, one, and multiple resource bands have been tested
for secretary workloads.

Do not infer claims from the locator beyond the digest-bounded material.

The three office-work actions are digest-directed encoding.

Resource count does not establish importance.

Resource count does not establish urgency.

Resource count does not establish authority.

An irreversible zero-resource act is still governed by
[[Reversibility as the Master Gate]].

Do not count the digest's explicit ESI non-resources as resources when describing
the source method.

Do not imply that [[Manchester Triage Architecture]] and ESI are the same system.

Use [[Satisficing]] to stop search after an option clears its threshold, not to
skip a known dependency.
