# Urgency and Importance

## Operating Summary

Do not treat the popular Eisenhower Matrix as Eisenhower doctrine.

The sourced 1954 speech contains a binary distinction attributed to an unnamed
former college president.

It does not contain a two-by-two grid.

The operationally useful finding is the Mere Urgency Effect.

Across five experiments, people selected unimportant tasks carrying artificial
urgency over important tasks with objectively better payoffs.

Score importance before and independently of any deadline signal.

Apply [[Reversibility as the Master Gate]] before either score.

## Source-Led Facts

Eisenhower's 1954 address attributes the urgency and importance line to an
unnamed former college president
([primary transcript](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/address-the-second-assembly-the-world-council-churches-evanston-illinois)).

The transcript states a binary distinction.

It does not state a grid.

The digest says the common attribution to J. Roscoe Miller misreads the
transcript because Miller was in the audience.

The digest dates the two-by-two treatment to Covey in 1989.

It also records an independent appearance of the idea in Hummel in 1967.

The digest assigns `folklore` confidence to the matrix.

Zhu, Yang, and Hsee report the Mere Urgency Effect in the Journal of Consumer
Research.

The digest records five experiments.

Participants chose unimportant tasks with artificial urgency over important
tasks with objectively superior payoffs.

Participants did so even when told the urgent task had the worse payoff.

The digest assigns `evidence-based` confidence to this underlying effect.

The digest does not supply the article's exact primary URL.

## Operating Procedure

The following procedure is synthesis directed by the digest.

1. Run the reversibility classification in [[Reversibility as the Master Gate]].
2. Hide deadline labels while making the first importance assessment.
3. State the payoff or consequence that makes the item important.
4. Record the importance assessment.
5. Restore the deadline information.
6. Determine whether the urgency is real or merely a short completion window.
7. Record the deadline source.
8. Compare the task against alternatives with better objective payoff.
9. Do not promote a task solely because it appears to expire sooner.
10. After priority is set, choose an autonomy level from [[Seven Delegation Levels]].
11. For non-urgent items, apply [[ESI Resource Counting]].
12. For estimates, consult [[Reference-Class Forecasting]].

If a task is both important and urgent, process it under the declared authority
and escalation rules.

If it is urgent but unimportant, name the lower payoff before deciding.

If it is important but not urgent, protect it from displacement by artificial
urgency.

## Boundaries

This note does not endorse the familiar quadrant labels.

It does not claim Eisenhower invented a matrix.

It does not claim J. Roscoe Miller authored the quoted distinction.

It does not turn the five experiments into a universal numeric correction.

Do not invent a primary URL for the Mere Urgency Effect article.

It does not make deadlines irrelevant.

A legal or operational deadline can still be material.

The procedure separates the deadline signal so its independent effect remains
visible.

Importance scoring is not a grant of authority.

Use [[Satisficing]] to stop comparison after a qualifying option is found.

Use [[Manchester Triage Architecture]] only as an ordered architecture, not as a
substitute for a domain-specific priority policy.
