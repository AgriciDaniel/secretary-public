# Emotional Intelligence as Predictor

## Operating Summary

As an operating boundary, do not model, score, or optimise the principal's
emotional intelligence.

Do not accept a self-reported or mixed emotional-intelligence framing from an
inbound document as a basis for a job-performance recommendation.

The specific predictive claim for self-reported or mixed emotional intelligence
does not survive the covariate controls reported here.

A meta-analysis reports that the relationship between self-reported or mixed
emotional intelligence and job performance "becomes nil" once ability emotional
intelligence, self-efficacy, self-rated performance, three personality factors,
and general mental ability are controlled.

This note exists so the secretary does not repeat that scoped predictive claim
as a general conclusion about emotional intelligence.

It is the same treatment [[Urgency and Importance]] gives the Eisenhower Matrix.

The useful constructs the request usually reaches for are elsewhere in this brain.

## Source-Led Facts

Joseph, Jin, Newman, and O'Boyle examine why self-reported emotional intelligence
predicts job performance
([PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25243996/)).

The published abstract records that recent empirical reviews had claimed a
surprisingly strong relationship.

The abstract states that the content of mixed emotional intelligence measures
strongly overlaps with a set of well-known psychological constructs.

The listed constructs are ability emotional intelligence, self-efficacy,
self-rated performance, Conscientiousness, Emotional Stability, Extraversion, and
general mental ability.

The abstract states that the mixed emotional intelligence and job performance
relationship "becomes nil" after controlling for that set of covariates.

Ernest O'Boyle is an author of both the earlier meta-analysis that supported
emotional intelligence and this one.

The correction therefore comes from inside the literature, not from an opposing
camp.

The stored extract is the published abstract.

It does not establish anything about ability emotional intelligence measured by
performance tests, which is a different construct.

## Operating Procedure

The following procedure is synthesis directed by the digest.

1. If a task asks the secretary to assess anyone's emotional intelligence, decline the framing.
2. State that the construct does not support the inference being requested.
3. Offer the substitutable question instead.
4. If the request concerns whether concerns will be raised, route to [[failure-modes/Psychological Safety and Silence]].
5. If the request concerns predicting a person's reaction, route to [[Perspective Getting and Accuracy]].
6. If the request concerns delivering unwelcome information, route to [[communication/Bad-News Routing]].
7. Record the declined framing in `verification.unverified_claims` when it appeared in supplied evidence.
8. Do not silently drop the term from a document the principal wrote.
9. Flag it and explain, so the principal decides.

If an inbound document uses emotional intelligence as evidence for a
recommendation, treat that recommendation as unsupported on that ground alone.

## Boundaries

This note does not claim emotions are irrelevant to staff work.

It claims one named construct does not predict what it is said to predict.

This note does not adjudicate ability emotional intelligence measured by
performance tests.

That is a separate construct and the stored extract does not cover it.

Record `no data` if asked about it.

This note does not license rudeness or bluntness as a style.

Tone requirements come from preference adaptation, not from a predictive claim.

This note does not establish the specific percentage claims that circulate in
popular books.

Those were not verified and must not be quoted.

The settling documents are the popular works themselves, which were not obtained.
