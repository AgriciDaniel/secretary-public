# Ministerial Decision Memo

## Operating Summary

Use the UK ministerial submission order to prepare a principal-facing decision
memo that can be understood quickly and signed after explicit decisions are resolved.

The sourced order begins with Issue, Recommendation, and Timing on one page, then
moves to Discussion, clearance, annexes, and copy list
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).

The Secretary adaptation is synthesis. It preserves that order while applying the
project's existing signature test and authority boundaries.

The memo must recommend an action, not merely report a topic. Where options are
offered, it must include the sourced Do Nothing option
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).

## Source-Led Facts

- The DWP ministerial submission template sets an Issue section capped at 100 words
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).
- Recommendation must state clearly what the minister is invited to do
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).
- Routine timing is typically five working days
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).
- An urgent submission must state the reason for urgency
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).
- Issue, Recommendation, and Timing belong on one page
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).
- Discussion is capped at two A4 pages in the source template
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).
- Discussion covers impact, financial implications, evidence, risks, handling,
  parliamentary implications, and legal implications
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).
- The template requires a Do Nothing option when options are offered
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).
- The target is a submission readable in under five minutes
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).
- Sourced failure modes include burying financial impact, over-copying, and relying
  on annexes that are not read ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/)).

## Operating Procedure

### 1. Issue

- Describe the decision, not the history of the subject.
- Stay within the sourced 100-word cap.
- Put any decision-changing contradiction in this opening section.
- Apply [[communication/BLUF and Numeric Writing Constraints]] to the opening.
- If the issue is bad news, follow [[communication/Bad-News Routing]].

### 2. Recommendation

- State one recommended action.
- Name exactly what the principal is invited to approve, reject, or direct.
- Keep analysis separate from the requested act.
- Do not imply that the Secretary can exercise the principal's authority.
- Route authority boundaries through [[ethics/_index]].

### 3. Timing

- Mark the submission ROUTINE or URGENT.
- For ROUTINE, use the sourced typical five-working-day expectation only when the
  ministerial-template context applies.
- For URGENT, state the reason, not just the label.
- Do not manufacture a date where the supplied evidence provides none.
- Link a material early-warning condition to [[communication/No-Surprises Protocol]].

### 4. Discussion

- Keep the section within the sourced two-A4-page maximum.
- Cover impact.
- Surface financial implications instead of burying them.
- Identify the evidence used and its confidence.
- State risks and handling.
- State parliamentary and legal implications when the supplied evidence supports them.
- Preserve dissent, applying [[failure-modes/_index]].
- Use [[judgment/_index]] for choice structure rather than inventing a recommendation rule.

### 5. Options

- Include Do Nothing whenever options are presented.
- State the consequence of each option only where evidence supports it.
- Mark inference as inference and synthesis as synthesis.
- Avoid adding alternatives merely to make the list look complete.

### 6. Clearance, annexes, and copy list

- Record the required clearance.
- Put decision-critical content in the main memo, not only in an annex.
- Use annexes for supporting detail.
- Keep the copy list limited to recipients who need the submission.
- Do not let copying imply approval or delegated authority.

## Decision Memo Template

1. **Issue, maximum 100 words:** the decision and material context.
2. **Recommendation:** the exact action requested from the principal.
3. **Timing:** ROUTINE, or URGENT with the reason.
4. **Discussion, maximum two A4 pages:** impact, finance, evidence, risks,
   handling, parliamentary implications, legal implications.
5. **Options:** include Do Nothing when options are offered.
6. **Clearance:** required reviewers and unresolved reservations.
7. **Annexes:** supporting detail only.
8. **Copy list:** necessary recipients only.

## Boundaries

- This format supports a decision but does not supply missing evidence.
- The five-working-day timing is a template convention, not a universal deadline.
- Readability in under five minutes is a source target, not proof of comprehension.
- A memo is not complete if material dissent has been compressed away.
- An annex cannot cure a misleading main paper.
- The format does not grant authority to send, sign, or commit.
- Use [[communication/I-PASS Briefing Schema]] when responsibility passes to a receiver.
