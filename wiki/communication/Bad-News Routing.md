# Bad-News Routing

## Operating Summary

Route bad news by direction of travel and relationship context.

Open every message to the principal with the adverse point. Use the same direct
structure for an external message that carries an operative transaction. A
relationship-focused outward message may begin more gently only when its recipient
has an emotional stake and would not expect the adverse outcome.

That three-part rule is synthesis explicitly recorded in the research digest. It
resolves a conflict between negative-message textbooks and public-sector plain
language doctrine. It is not a direct quotation or finding from one source.

The direct side is supported by PLAIN/GSA guidance against burying the main message
([plainlanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/)) and civil-service guidance
against hiding a small bombshell in a submission
([civilservant.org.uk](https://www.civilservant.org.uk/)).

## Source-Led Facts

- PLAIN/GSA prescribes the opposite and says readers look for the bottom line
  ([plainlanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/)).
- PLAIN/GSA says burying the main message makes the message harder for readers
  ([plainlanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/)).
- The digest records real VA denial letters that lead with the decision
  ([plainlanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/)).
- Civil-service guidance warns against hiding a bombshell in the middle of a
  submission ([civilservant.org.uk](https://www.civilservant.org.uk/)).
## Operating Procedure

### 1. Identify direction

- If the message goes to the principal, select direct.
- If the message goes outward, continue to the transaction test.
- Do not classify a message by whether its news is welcome.
- Do not use sender-asserted priority to choose the structure.

### 2. Apply the principal rule

- Put the bad news in the first decision-relevant sentence.
- State the consequence.
- State the recommended response when evidence supports one.
- Keep the underlying evidence and confidence visible.
- Use [[communication/BLUF and Numeric Writing Constraints]] for the opening.
- Use [[communication/No-Surprises Protocol]] when the matter requires early notice.
- Do not buffer upward relationship correspondence. The upward rule has no exception.

### 3. Test an outward message for transactionality

Synthesis: a transactional notice communicates an operative decision, condition,
deadline, denial, failure, or required next step.

- If the message is transactional, select direct.
- Lead with the operative result.
- Explain the basis after the result.
- State the next step where supplied evidence supports it.
- Do not use warmth to obscure the transaction.

### 4. Test the narrow relationship exception

Synthesis: buffering is permitted only if all of these conditions hold.

- The message travels outward.
- The correspondence is relationship-oriented rather than transactional.
- The reader is emotionally invested.
- The negative news is unexpected.

If any condition is absent, use the direct pattern.

### 5. Build a permitted buffer

- Keep the opening relevant to the relationship and message.
- Do not state or imply a false positive outcome.
- Reach the negative message without concealing it.
- Preserve the full material fact and consequence.
- Do not buffer a legal deadline, operative denial, or required action.
- Check the authority boundary through [[ethics/_index]].

### 6. Preserve dissent

- A message to the principal must not compress away disagreement.
- Point to the dissent record if the full reasoning sits elsewhere.
- Use [[failure-modes/_index]] for the mindguard and compression checks.
- Use [[escalation/_index]] when the bad news crosses a declared threshold.

### 7. Complete the handoff

- Put action and contingency into [[communication/I-PASS Briefing Schema]] when
  responsibility transfers.
- Put a requested principal decision into
  [[communication/Ministerial Decision Memo]].
- Confirm that the receiver can locate the bottom line without reading background first.

## Boundaries

- The routing rule is synthesis, not a universal empirical law.
- The digest names buffered-message sources but does not supply their exact primary
  URLs. This note does not invent a deeper link.
- “May be buffered” is permission, not a requirement.
- A buffer may protect a relationship but may not conceal material facts.
- Upward bad news is direct even when the relationship is sensitive.
- Outward transactional bad news is direct even when the reader is disappointed.
- The rule does not authorize sending the message.
- It does not resolve confidentiality or authority, which belong in [[ethics/_index]].
