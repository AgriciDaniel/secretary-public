# No-Surprises Protocol

## Operating Summary

Use early notice for material matters when the supplied evidence supports it.
The previously cited Crown Law document is cited-but-unlinked, so its specific
receipt conditions and significance test are no data. The verified New Zealand
Public Service Commission page supports free and frank advice, not a substitute
for the missing Crown Law text. This protocol is contested local synthesis.

## Source-Led Facts

The Public Service Commission page is a verified analogue for free and frank
advice. The repository has no reachable primary record for the Crown Law
no-surprises claim, its paragraph numbers, or its detailed receipt conditions.
The FRC UK Corporate Governance Code was checked for the phrase and does not
supply this doctrine.

## Operating Procedure

1. Identify the matter and the evidence that makes it material.
2. Lead with verified fact, inference, and uncertainty.
3. State why the principal is receiving the notice.
4. Name every supplied restriction on publication or discussion.
5. State the expected response, or say that no response is expected.
6. Do not convert notification into implied approval or consultation.
7. Route missing authority or capability under [[escalation/_index]].
8. Preserve the evidence and visible dissent with the record.

### Evidence review

Before routing a notice, check that the source actually supports each factual
statement. If a condition comes only from the unavailable Crown Law record, label
it no data rather than importing it by implication. Do not use an official
publisher's reputation as a substitute for a claim-level document.

### Follow-up

If the principal requests a decision, use [[communication/Ministerial Decision Memo]].
If the work transfers between owners, use [[communication/I-PASS Briefing Schema]].
If a material objection appears, use [[communication/Bad-News Routing]] and
[[failure-modes/Mindguard and Compression]]. If no action is approved, retain the
notice as awareness rather than marking it complete.

## Boundaries

Do not attribute this protocol's detailed steps to Crown Law until the primary
record is captured. A notice does not grant authority, override a legal
restriction, or erase an independent function's responsibilities. Do not invent
numeric significance thresholds.
